# Parting Thoughts 

Through the course of this section, we've studied a host of different
techniques in differentiable physics ranging across new featurizations,
differentiable representations, equivariant architectures,
differentiable simulators and more. Very broadly, we can separate out
these techniques into two classes

-   **Anchored in the Physics**: These techniques leverage
    differentiable programming methods on the set of governing laws of
    the system to make existing physical algorithms tunable and
    learnable.

-   **From Geometry**: These methods add rich invariances, leveraging
    known facts about group invariances to learn more rigidly
    constrained representations directly from deep learning
    architectures?

Do these two classes of methods meet in the middle although they
originate from seemingly entirely different basis? For every physical
algorithm, can we learn a suitable invariance structure that enables us
to bypass classical algorithmic structures? Or will it prove more
effective to continue to transmute classical numerical codes into
differentiable programs?

Differentiable Physics as a field is in its earlier infancy, and the
answers to these and many more questions remain mysterious. We hope that
you have learned the foundations of the field through this book and now
emerge ready to tackle fundamental questions about the physical reality.
Happy questing!
