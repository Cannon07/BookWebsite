A tensor of type 
\begin{align}
\big ( \begin{smallmatrix} 0 \\ N \end{smallmatrix} \big )
\end{align}
is a function that maps a collection of $N$ vectors into a real number. This function must be linear in each argument. For example, the inner product $\cdot$ is a $\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0 \\ 2 \end{smallmatrix} \big)$ tensor since

\begin{align}
(\alpha \vec{v}) \cdot \vec{w} &= \alpha (\vec{v} \cdot \vec{w}) \\
(\vec{v} + \vec{w}) \cdot \vec{u} &= \vec{v} \cdot \vec{u} + \vec{w} \cdot \vec{u} 
\end{align}
(Since the inner product is symmetric, the same properties hold true in the other argument). The \textit{metric} tensor is defined to be the tensor corresponding to the inner product
\begin{align}
g(\vec{v}, \vec{w}) = \vec{v} \cdot \vec{w}
\end{align}

As another example, if $f$ is a function, then its total derivative $df$ is a $\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0 \\ 1 \end{smallmatrix}\big)$ tensor (also called a one-form). More generally, a one form is a function from vectors to the real numbers.

Note that one-forms form a vector space since functions can be added or multiplied by a scalar. We can also talk about "components" of the one-form as where they map basis vectors
\begin{align}
p_{\alpha} &= \tilde{p}(\vec{e}_{\alpha})
\end{align}
